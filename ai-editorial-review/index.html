What I look for

Facts and sources

Unsupported claims, questionable statistics, missing attribution, and quotations that cannot be traced to the original material.

Structure and voice

Repetition, generic openings, weak conclusions, inflated language, and prose that sounds nothing like you.

I edit the draft as an editor. The service does not include AI detection scores or promises to pass detection tools.

Before

“In today’s ever-changing digital landscape, our conservation organization is proud to announce the launch of its monthly newsletter, a testament to our unwavering commitment to fostering meaningful connections. This innovative publication will serve as a vital resource, keeping readers informed about our important work.”
  • The opening delays the actual news.
  • “Innovative” and “vital” add claims without support.
  • The reader still does not know what the newsletter contains.

After

“Our new monthly newsletter will keep you informed about our conservation work.”

The edit makes the announcement clear and cuts the inflated language. Before publication, I would ask what the newsletter covers and how readers can subscribe. Those details belong in the copy once supplied.
